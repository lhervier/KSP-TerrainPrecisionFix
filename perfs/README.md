# Performance measurement

What the fix costs, and the material to reproduce it. Throwaway, like `Src/Bench.cs`: both go away once
the figures are in the main README.

`Src/Bench.cs` records in memory and writes nothing until asked. `benchMode` and `patchEnabled` live in
`GameData/TerrainPrecisionFixMod/PluginData/settings.cfg`; in flight, Alt+F8 dumps to `KSP.log` and
Alt+F7 starts over.

## The saves

`saves/bench-mun-NNkm.sfs`: a craft in a circular equatorial orbit of the Mun, one file per altitude.
Copy them into a save folder (they were built for `saves/claude`), load the game, then Alt+F9.

They are generated by `make_bench_saves.py` from a `persistent.sfs` holding the craft on the launch pad.
Only that craft's VESSEL node is rewritten — situation and orbital elements — plus `scene = 7` so the
game opens in flight rather than at the space centre. All the files share one `UT`, and every orbit has
`MNA = 0` and `EPH = UT`, so they all start at the same point of the same trajectory: each is identical
from one load to the next, and they are comparable to each other.

There is nothing to fly. The craft carries a Terrier and a full tank only so that an orbit that turns out
to be wrong can be corrected in place instead of reloading.

## What the terrain does, measured

Logged by the mod itself, once per sphere, whenever `benchMode` is not `off`.

| sphere | minLevel | maxLevel | highest level appears under | lowest level with a collider | max angle per sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kerbin | 2 | 10 | 9 375 m | 10 | 4.60e-5 rad |
| Mun | 2 | 9 | 6 250 m | 9 | 9.20e-5 rad |
| KerbinOcean | 2 | 7 | 75 000 m | none | 3.68e-4 rad |

Two things follow, and both matter beyond this folder:

- **`maxLevelOffset` is 0** on Kerbin and on the Mun, so only the highest subdivision level carries a
  collider — the very level the fix acts on. It covers every quad a craft can stand on. (TODO, *Colliders
  below the highest level*.)
- **The fix only runs below 6 250 m over the Mun**, and 9 375 m over Kerbin. Higher up, the game never
  builds the highest level at all, so a flight up there exercises none of the patched code.

The second limit fights the first: the lower the orbit, the faster the craft crosses the ground, and past
`max angle per sample` the game refuses to subdivide that far (`PQ.UpdateSubdivision` only splits a quad
while `subdivision < sphereRoot.maxLevelAtCurrentTgtSpeed`). Measured at 10 km, `speedLevelCap` sat at 9
the whole time — a low Mun orbit is nowhere near that ceiling,
which leaves the altitude as the only real constraint.

## Runs

Each file in `runs/` is the `BENCH` lines of one dump, with the log prefix stripped. `-fix` is
`patchEnabled = true`, `-stock` is the same DLL and the same save with that one line flipped.

### `mun-10km-fix` — negative control

110 samples in steady flight, 118 fps, 10.9 quads built per second, 2.71 ms per quad (12.0 µs per vertex,
every PQSMod included). Terrain cost 7.5 % of real time, 0.63 ms per frame, of which building quads was
2.9 %.

**`patchedQuads` is 0 over the whole run** and `subdivisionMax` never passes 8: at 10 km the craft stays
above the 6 250 m threshold. Nothing here measures the fix. Kept because it is the control that shows
what the counters read when the patch is idle — and because 12 µs of stock work per vertex is the
denominator the patch will have to be compared against.
